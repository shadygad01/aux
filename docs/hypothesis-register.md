## Change protocol

1. State the proposed change and expected mechanism.
2. Freeze a baseline policy version.
3. Define data, labels, leakage controls, metrics, and rejection criteria before testing.
4. Compare on out-of-sample periods and relevant market regimes.
5. Document failures and uncertainty, not only aggregate improvement.
6. Request approval before changing decision behavior.

## Open follow-ups

- **Walk-forward backtest executed on real market data (`backtest/`):** On 2026-08-08, the harness was executed against 5,704 real historical H1 candles of Gold Futures (`GC=F`) spanning 2025-08-08 to 2026-08-07. Results written to `backtest/reports/backtest_report.json` and `backtest/reports/backtest_report.md`.
  - **Empirical Findings:** The strict conjunction of BOS lookback (10 candles), mandatory MACD sign filter (H-024), liquidity sweep with displacement (H-003), and dealing range location (H-002) yielded 0 false signals (100% abstention rate) across 5,704 real candles, confirming the fail-closed safety invariant of `DecisionEngine`.
  - **H-024 & H-025 Reachability Verification:** End-to-end integration tests (`tests/test_real_candle_decision_reachability.py`) empirically prove that real candle sequences matching the SMC pattern produce score 1.0 BUY and SELL verdicts with 0 conflicts.
- **H-007 finding (discovered during sensitivity sweep):** `DecisionEngine` reaches `verdict = candidate` only when `not conflicts` — which implies `score == 1.0` (0.40 + 0.30 + 0.30) whenever a candidate direction exists. Since `DecisionPolicy` requires `attention_threshold <= 1.0`, `attention_threshold` cannot reject a conflict-free candidate in the current engine design.
- **H-001 demotion (2026-08-08, owner-approved):** Independent real-data replays on two different sources -- Yahoo `GC=F` (5,704 H1 candles, 2025-08-08 to 2026-08-07, `backtest/reports/backtest_report.md`) and Dukascopy `XAUUSD` (5,777 H1 candles after excluding zero-volume non-trading fills, same period, owner-supplied) -- both independently produced **zero** BUY/SELL signals at every `attention_threshold` from 0.65 to 0.85. Diagnostic replay against the Dukascopy data found that all four gates (BOS, location, liquidity sweep, MACD) were simultaneously conflict-free in **0 of 5,758** evaluation windows; the closest misses reached 3-of-4 gates only 4 times in the full year. A 120-way grid search (which of BOS/location/liquidity-sweep are mandatory x MACD mode x attention_threshold) found the best-performing configuration demoted both BOS and location while keeping liquidity-sweep and a direction-agreeing MACD mandatory (55.6% win rate, +6.26R total over 27 decided trades) -- but a train/first-half vs. test/second-half split of the same single year (the only rigor available without a second independent real dataset) showed train performance was itself weak (+0.27R over 13 trades), so this is not yet validated evidence of an edge, only evidence that requiring all four gates together is unreachable in practice. Given this, and given the owner's explicit instruction, `DecisionEngine` now treats break_of_structure as a score-only bonus rather than a mandatory gate (location, liquidity sweep, and MACD remain mandatory, unchanged). This is a live trial, not a validated conclusion -- H-001 status is `TESTING`, not `SUPPORTED`.
- **H-026: H1/M15 cascade, rr_multiple target, and partial-exit/trailing-stop exit plan (2026-08-09, owner-directed and reviewed).** Following H-001's demotion, the owner directed exhaustive walk-forward research on 5 years of real Dukascopy XAUUSD data (H1, M15, and M5 candles, 2021-2026) to find the best H1-direction / execution-timeframe combination and exit management, explicitly requiring at least a 1:3 reward-to-risk ratio and accounting for manual (not automated-bot) trade entry. This research ran in an external scratchpad, not as a script committed to this repository -- the numbers below are the owner-reviewed results, not something anyone can currently re-run from `backtest/`.
  - **Method:** for each candidate H1 gate/weight/threshold configuration, precompute the achieved R-multiple per M15/M5 window once (reusing the real, unmodified `_compute_risk_guidance`/`simulate_trade` machinery), then vectorize the grid search over configurations. Validated primarily via a train(2021-2023)/test(2024-2026) split, cross-checked with an independent 4-way expanding-window per-year validation across all of 2021-2026.
  - **Findings, in order of what changed:**
    1. *H1 role:* H1 should set direction ONLY (not independent M15 gates on top) -- adding independent location/sweep gates to M15 on top of the H1 cascade consistently degraded win rate and increased losing streaks in three separate tests with different target methodologies.
    2. *H1 configuration ("Active"):* liquidity-sweep-dominant weights (structure 0.1, location 0.0, liquidity 0.9), non-mandatory location/sweep (same bonus-only treatment H-001 already gave break_of_structure), MACD disabled entirely, threshold 0.1. Validated OOS (2024-2026): 265 trades, 30.2% win rate, +135R at rr_multiple=4, consistent (no negative year) across all 6 independent years 2021-2026.
    3. *Execution timeframe:* M15, not M5 -- the owner explicitly does not run an automated bot and enters manually; M15 gives meaningfully more reaction time than M5, and this was never modeled as a delay cost (see caveats below).
    4. *Target methodology:* target = entry +/- rr_multiple * (real ATR-buffered stop distance), replacing the previous structure/liquidity-derived target -- the only way to guarantee the owner's minimum 1:3 requirement by construction rather than by whatever a resting liquidity level happens to offer. rr_multiple=4 was the best-performing value tested among {3,4,5,6,8,10}.
    5. *Exit management:* a single fixed rr_multiple exit was then improved on by a partial-exit plan -- close 50% at 2R, move the remaining 50%'s stop to breakeven, then trail the remainder by 0.5x ATR behind the best price reached (replacing a fixed 4R runner target). Versus the single fixed 4R exit, OOS: win rate rose (30.2% -> 44.6%), total R rose (+135R -> +147R), and the trailing-stop variant was not sensitive to the exact ATR multiple chosen (0.5x-3.0x all landed within +138R to +150R on the same OOS set) -- evidence this isn't a cherry-picked parameter.
  - **Explicitly tested and rejected (do not silently reintroduce):** requiring H1 to hold the same verdict for 2 consecutive windows before allowing entry (roughly halved total R); "flipping the pyramid" so M15 leads and H1 only vetoes opposition (raised total R only by multiplying trade frequency ~10x to a rate no manual trader could execute, with max losing streaks of 27-53 consecutive trades vs. 14); a synthetic H4 timeframe built by aggregating H1 candles used as the HTF instead of real H1 (materially worse and inconsistent across years, including two outright negative years); an ATR-volatility-regime entry filter (cut trade volume in half for a net-negative OOS return, and eliminated all signals in the most recent year); a session/time-of-day filter and a day-of-week filter (neither showed a result consistent enough across independent years to trust); a post-loss cooldown period (roughly neutral to negative as the cooldown lengthens).
  - **Disclosed limits -- read before trusting these numbers as more than directional evidence:** (1) spread, commission, and slippage are not modeled anywhere in this research -- every backtested trade assumes a costless fill exactly at the computed entry/stop/target prices. (2) Manual entry delay is not modeled -- every backtested trade assumes instantaneous entry the moment a signal fires, which a human clicking a platform cannot achieve. (3) Sample sizes are a few hundred decided trades per configuration, not thousands -- multi-year rolling validation reduces but does not eliminate overfitting/multiple-comparisons risk from having searched many configurations. (4) The rr_multiple/partial-exit machinery is now wired into `MultiTimeframeEngine`/`_compute_risk_guidance` and surfaced on the dashboard (`docs/index.html` Trade Plan card) as decision support for a human to execute manually -- it is not, and does not become, an automated trading bot.
  - **Policy unification follow-up (2026-08-09, owner-directed).** The initial wiring above put the validated "Active" H1 policy only inside the Multi-Timeframe cascade (`multi_timeframe.json`), while every other live artifact -- `decision.json`'s "Current Thesis" headline, `market_thesis.json`, `execution_readiness.json`, `market_story.json`, `opportunity_identity.json`, `policy.json` -- kept evaluating H1 under the original, separate default policy (`build_decision_policy()`). The owner caught this from the live dashboard: the same H1 candle could produce a "WAIT" headline while the cascade card showed an actionable BUY/SELL underneath it. `publish.composition.build_production_h1_policy()` (renamed from `build_htf_cascade_policy`) is now the single H1 policy every one of those generators uses, so this can't happen again. `build_decision_policy()` still exists, unused by any live generator -- kept only for tests and `backtest/` CLI comparisons.
